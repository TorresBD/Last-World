## 2. TRUTHFUL REPORTING

Use only these four status words, and use them precisely:

- **planned**: written down, no code
- **implemented**: code exists and compiles
- **tested**: a check ran and passed; name the check
- **measured**: numbers recorded on named hardware; give the numbers

- Never claim an FPS, pawn count, speed or memory result you did not measure on this project's real workload.

[LW027] SECTION  4. ENGINEERING INVARIANTS (never break these)

## 4. ENGINEERING INVARIANTS (never break these)

1. **One owner per quantity.** Each animal, stock, resource or item is owned by exactly one place. No duplicated populations, cargo or biomass.
2. **Integer counts are exact.** Births, deaths, transfers and migrations conserve totals. Use checked arithmetic. No negative counts, no silent clamping.
3. **One game clock.** A single integer tick counter drives everything. Frame rate must never change game outcomes.
4. **Unseen does not mean frozen.** Offscreen ecology and combat keep progressing at coarse resolution.
5. **Information filtering.** Cameras, radar and satellite limit what the player sees. No UI, inspector or tooltip may leak hidden positions, species or health.
6. **CPU is authoritative for gameplay rules first.** GPU is for rendering and bulk work, and only becomes authoritative after end-to-end validation.
7. **No full-world fine-resolution allocations.** Use chunks, pages and sparse data. Track memory high-water marks.
8. **Stale jobs are rejected.** Background results must carry revision stamps and be validated before commit.
9. **Saves are sacred.** Never break an existing save without a migration. Use atomic writes and keep the previous save.
10. **Extinction is permanent** unless real surviving organisms or viable stored stages exist.
11. **Every trait has a real effect, cost and test.** No label-only traits.
12. **Determinism.** Same generator version, seed and settings must give the same accepted world.

[LW115] SECTION  Revised selection rationale

## Revised selection rationale

The first slice should reveal expensive structural assumptions before later traits are added: a river must be able to back up; water must share space with roads, banks, foundations and boats; underground shelter must have actual connected shape and finite occupancy; vegetation and animals must be able to alter habitat persistently. Family quotas are therefore removed. Basic sensing, ordinary hunting and mandatory care remain baseline work even when a specialized catalogue mechanism is not selected here.

The list includes direct terrain/habitat engineering (12, 46, 65, 67, 101–105, 156–157), changes to soil/water/resource fields (102, 121, 159), and the living responses necessary to make those changes meaningful (migration, dormancy, flood tolerance, regeneration, seed transport and pollination). A dam is not merely an obstruction sprite, and a burrow is not a surface dot that makes an animal invulnerable.

[LW119] SECTION  Coupled interactions the first architecture must support

## Coupled interactions the first architecture must support

1. **Dam → reservoir → changed habitat:** construction consumes real wood/other suitable material; reduced channel outflow retains volume, raises upstream surface and inundates connected low banks. Downstream flow changes according to leakage/spill/other tributaries, not an unconditional dry-river flag. Wetland habitat, drowning risk, plant survival, river crossings and vessel clearance respond to the resulting fields.
2. **Breach → release → erosion/deposition:** removal or failure opens conductance and releases finite stored volume over time. Sediment/soil changes stay local and bounded; downstream terrain/route updates must propagate through connected regions even outside the viewport. Visual water is not the sole effect.
3. **Bent burrow → finite refuge → flood/collapse:** passages contain entrances, centerline segments and chambers with radius/height. Underground travel follows those segments, not world-surface routing. A water connection can flood a lower chamber; an explosion cutting its roof may open or collapse a section. Occupants have explicit escape, blockage, displacement or death outcomes. No free teleport to the closest surface cell.
4. **Animals → soil → vegetation → flow:** mixing, wallowing, pits and tree thinning alter substrate/cover; cover/root loss changes infiltration/runoff/erosion susceptibility. Growth and litter then modify cover/nutrients over time. This is a bounded gameplay model with finite stocks, not a promise of a full geotechnical/fluid simulation.
5. **Reef → habitat and naval clearance:** living builders gradually create persistent underwater structure. Water height and solid-bed/reef height jointly determine available depth for each draft class. A reef can survive as dead substrate after its builders die; removal/fragmentation must explicitly move or destroy material.
6. **Season → hydrology → physiology and reproduction:** Tor approved one full seasonal cycle per hour at1x. Temperature/rainfall/evaporation and biome-specific season envelopes affect soil, river supply, ground traction, plant outputs, dormancy, migration, incubation and breeding. Not every jungle receives snow or identical four-season labels. These effects derive from the same clock and remain active offscreen.
7. **Knowledge-safe explanation:** flooded ground visible to a camera can be shown; an unobserved underground chamber and its inhabitants cannot. Dots on radar remain unidentified contacts and do not reveal subsurface geometry. Observed entrance/status cues and confidence can explain ecology without granting omniscience.

## Shared ownership and scheduling

`Ecology` owns animal/cohort identities, plant patches, food, reproductive tickets, body/reserve material, biological water and deaths. `HabitatWorld` owns sparse physical geometry, finite occupancy references and built material; it never owns a second animal. `EcosystemPhysics` owns finite underground substrate, two soil-mixing layers, mineral sources, work queues and engineering wallets. `Terrain`/`GroundModel` own surface cut/fill and soil/sediment; `WaterGraph` owns surface liters and fluxes. Source cohorts are materialized exactly once for individual physical work, with actual species body width. Construction can wait or fail for lack of material, time, space or access; it does not silently create resources.

Engineering uses prepared habitat transactions and source preflight. Harvested wood/fiber leaves `Ecology::export_biomass` once; it is not simultaneously standing biomass, litter and timber. Underground excavation removes finite underground substrate and produces spoil without lowering the entire surface. Fine pit/wallow excavation retains the sub-geometry residual so millimeter terrain quantization neither destroys nor invents soil. The sparse maps and pending routes survive serialization.

Trait flags use five32-bit words for160 independent flags. Numeric profiles, prerequisites, partners, delays and reserves remain separate. [LW164] SECTION  ADDITIONAL COMPATIBILITY RULES

ADDITIONAL COMPATIBILITY RULES

Treat partner-dependent traits as real relationships: pollination needs matching plants and animals; brood parasites need appropriate hosts; fungus farmers need a suitable crop; mimicry needs a relevant model and perceiving predators. Soaring requires flight, and gliding requires elevation and appropriate anatomy. Regeneration is limited to eligible body parts. Rare reproductive methods must follow their own viability and inheritance rules rather than becoming universal rescue abilities.

Distinguish species capabilities from current state: a toxin-capable animal may have depleted reserves; a sperm-storing animal may have no stored sperm. Poison and venom use different exposure routes. Renewable animal products consume nutrition and time, and harvesting can compete with defense, insulation or care of young. A trait must never imply immunity, unlimited output or guaranteed success.
